Lupus may be driven by faulty mRNA splicing in neutrophils

Neutrophils in most lupus patients make aberrantly spliced transcripts whose peptides act as neoantigens, recognized by patients’ T cells and autoantibodies but largely absent in healthy donors. It points to a new autoimmune mechanism and possible drug target.

ScienceScience & CS

  • Lupus: Nature paper finds cryptic, aberrantly spliced mRNA in neutrophils of most lupus patients creates peptide neoantigens shown on MHC class I. They expand patient T cells and bind IgG autoantibodies, and are enriched in interferon-inducible genes. Clinical validation is still needed. Nature, UW Medicine
  • Physics: “stupendously” large black holes are ruled out as dark matter, since they would have left a bigger imprint on cosmic radiation (Gerlach et al., PRL). Nature
